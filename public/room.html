    Who’s joining?

    Your friends in this meetup will see this name on your pin.

    Next, your browser asks for location. Allowing it only puts you on your map — nothing reaches your friends until you press Send my location.

    How Meet Up works

    A meetup is one map that everybody with the link can see.

    Three steps

    1. Send my location. Your pin goes on the map. Nothing leaves this device before you press it.
    2. Share link. Whoever opens it joins this same map and picks a name.
    3. Watch them land. Pins update live, and the panel shows how far away everyone is.

    What other people see

    • Only people with the link — the room code is random, but it is not a password.
    • Your position while you are sharing, and nothing after you press Stop sharing. That wipes your coordinates from the server.
    • A grey, dashed pin marked paused once you switch away from this tab, so a stale position never passes for a live one.

    If location is blocked

    Browsers remember a refusal, so the prompt will not come back on its own. A private window counts as a fresh browser and asks again.

    • Desktop Click the icon at the left of the address bar → Location → Allow, then reload.
    • iPhone Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services → your browser → While Using the App, then reload.
    • Android Tap the lock icon in the address bar → Permissions → Location, then reload.
